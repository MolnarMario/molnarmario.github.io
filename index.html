02 / Process

How I build

I've sat in every seat a product passes through, from the first idea to the support inbox after launch. You get one person who keeps the whole picture in mind, and a modern toolchain that makes it fast without cutting corners.

  1. 1

    Start with the problem

    Before any code, I want to know who it's for, what they're trying to do and what would make them come back. A good idea survives those questions. A weak one gets fixed cheaply, on paper.

  2. 2

    Agree on what done looks like

    What it has to do, what it won't do, and how we'll check it works. All of it written down and agreed before the build starts, so nothing at handover comes as a surprise.

  3. 3

    Build in short loops

    Work is split into small, parallel tasks, with AI-assisted tooling doing the heavy lifting. You see real progress mid-build, and your feedback goes straight into the next loop, not a list for later.

  4. 4

    Test it twice

    Testing has been my day job for years. Automated tests run on every change, and AI Hive's suite alone makes over 4,000 checks. Then I use it myself, on real devices, the way your users will. Green tests catch regressions. They don't catch a confusing screen.

  5. 5

    Ship something you can open

    A link that plays, an APK that installs, an extension that loads. If it only runs on my machine, it isn't done.

  6. 6

    Listen after launch

    Release is where the real feedback starts. Years in support taught me to talk to the people using the thing, find what confuses them and turn that into the next round of work.
