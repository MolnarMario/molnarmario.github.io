Ironlog

The powerlifting tracker I use for my own training. No account, and nothing leaves your browser.

Open the app ↗ Get started Source on GitHub ↗ Commit log

What it does

What I need from a training app, in the order I use it.

01

Log the session at the rack

Between heavy sets I have about a minute and chalky hands. Logging has to be quick, and it has to capture how the session felt, not only the numbers.

  • Set by set. Weight and reps with RPE or a percentage of your max, per-set gear and notes, from a 47-lift exercise library plus your own.
  • How you came in. Readiness meters for sleep, motivation, hydration, calories, protein and stimulants.
  • Session timing. Start and end times are tracked for you, or you enter the duration by hand.

02

Know which plates go on the bar

Working out the plates for an odd weight after a hard set is how you load the bar wrong.

  • The loader draws the bar. It solves the plates per side, with bar and collar options and kg micro plates.
  • One tap from the log. The plate icon on any logged set sends that weight straight to the loader.
  • Real plates in either unit. In lb mode the bar is 45 lb and the plates are 45s, 35s and 25s, not converted kilos.

03

Food on the same calendar

A bad week of training often starts with a bad week of eating. So food lives in the same app, on the same calendar as the lifting.

  • Track food against your targets. Calories and macros against daily targets from a 92-food pantry plus your own foods, with water and bodyweight.
  • Scan a label. Photograph a package and the app finds the nutrition table and reads it on your phone. A barcode, when there is one, is looked up on Open Food Facts first.
  • Training and food on one calendar. The month view can overlay each day's calories, macros and bodyweight on the training grid.

04

See the trend

A log is only worth keeping if it tells you something back.

  • Progress per lift. Estimated one-rep max and top set over time, and your recent records.
  • Volume and consistency. Weekly volume, weekly sets per muscle group, your week streak, and bodyweight against volume.
  • Food trends. Average calories, protein and water, macro composition and bodyweight over time.

05

Your data stays yours

There is no account and no server, so keeping the data safe is entirely the app's job.

  • Switch kg and lb freely. Weights are stored in kilograms and converted for display, so changing units never rewrites your history.
  • Your old data comes along. On first load it offers to import data from the older single-file version, and it never deletes the original.
  • It tells you when saving fails. If the browser refuses to store data, in private mode or with full storage, a banner says so instead of failing silently.

Get started

Ironlog runs in the browser, on a phone or a computer, with no account and nothing to install. Your data stays in that browser.

  1. Open the app. Go to ironlog.thebarbellengineer.com. On a phone, add it to your home screen.
  2. Set your maxes. Enter your squat, bench and deadlift one-rep maxes, so percentage-based sets work out their weights.
  3. Log a session. Under Workout, Today, pick an exercise and log your sets.
  4. Load the bar. Tap the plate icon on a set to see it on the bar in Plates.
  5. Track food. Set daily targets under Food, Goals, log what you eat, and turn on + Food in the workout calendar to see both together.

How it was built

It began on 11 June 2026 as a single 424 KB HTML file holding a minified React bundle. In August I rebuilt it as a Vite, React and TypeScript project, one tab at a time, with the old build kept as the reference for how everything should behave.

Decisions that mattered

  • Kilograms underneath. Weights are stored in kilograms and converted for display, so switching units never rewrites your history.
  • Never lose the old data. On first load it offers to import data from the original build, reports how many days and sets came across, names any exercise it couldn't match and never deletes the original.
  • Parity you can check. Every class in the original stylesheet was diffed against the new code. 17 are unused, and all 17 were already dead in the original.
  • Say so when saving fails. If the browser refuses to store data, in private mode or with full storage, a banner says so instead of failing silently.

How it's tested

Unit tests cover the plate solver, the food maths, the statistics behind every chart, reading a nutrition label and the import from the old build. Every build runs the TypeScript typecheck first.

Where it stands

In use for my own training. The workout, plates and food tabs are all rebuilt.
