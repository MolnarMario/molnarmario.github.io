0.4.0 A real vocabulary, kanji reference, and honest scores

Aug 2, 2026

Added

  • The word list grew from about 250 words to nearly 700. The hiragana deck now carries the common JLPT N5 vocabulary, plus 25 more katakana loanwords, so practising words no longer runs out of material.
  • A "Kanji" button on words that have a kanji spelling (off by default; turn on "Kanji reference" in Study scope). It is colored by how the word is normally written: green when it is usually kanji, blue when it is mixed, grey when it is usually kana. Tapping it opens a reference card with the kanji, the reading, the meaning, and, for words that sound alike, the other kanji that share that reading with a one-line note on the difference. Purely informative: it is never quizzed and never scored.
  • Filter the Words tab by category (hiragana), with removable chips, both when browsing and when building a word set.
  • A "From my strong kana" set generator. It builds a word set out of words whose every kana you already score 85 or better in both writing and reading, so review material comes from what you have actually learned.

Changed

  • Writing mode now opens the Library instead of a random character. Picking what to practise is the first thing you do, which is how Reading mode already worked. Tapping a character or word drops you straight into practice, and Back returns to the Library.
  • Handwriting scores are a measurement now, not a cap. Two scores used to be suspiciously common: a well-formed character the recognizer merely misread always landed on exactly 65, and a scribble that cleared a low bar always landed on exactly 45. Both were ceilings rather than measurements. Recognition now nudges the score instead of capping it, extra strokes actually cost something, no scoring mode is blind to the shape any more, and a backwards stroke is charged once rather than twice. A clean katakana "e" that used to show 65 with a "looks more like" complaint now scores 93; a single random line scores 7 instead of 45.
  • The score on the feedback card and the score in the Library finally agree. The Library used to average the raw form dial while the card showed the adjusted number, so the same attempt could read 65 on one screen and 88 on the other. It now stores and averages the number you were actually shown. Practice recorded before this update keeps counting toward your averages.
  • Katakana are no longer misread as the kanji they came from. Thirty lookalike pairs (the katakana "e" and the kanji for "work", and so on) are now treated as the same drawing, because nothing in the ink can tell them apart.
  • The reading quiz keeps a steady layout. The prompt no longer jumps when you answer, typing works the way it does in flashcard practice (the keyboard stays open, the Done key checks and moves on, an empty answer skips), and memory-hint stories bold the reading they are teaching.

0.3.3 Smarter Library search and visible scores

Aug 2, 2026

Added

  • Your writing score now shows on each word in the Library list. Words in the Characters/Words list already tinted green by score; now they also print the number ("Avg 82 · Best 90", or "Not practiced yet"), matching the grid view and the character rows.
  • Reading progress keeps the romaji alongside it. In Reading mode the word list used to replace a word's romaji with its progress stats; it now shows the romaji as usual and puts the progress on its own line, so you never lose the reading.

Changed

  • Library search ranks results by relevance. An exact match now floats to the top instead of being buried under incidental substring hits, so searching "hat" finds the word "hat" first rather than "that" and "what". Romaji search works the same way, with or without spaces ("boushi" or "bo u shi").
  • The reading-progress line labels the typed-answer score "Typing" instead of the terser "Type".

0.3.1 Quiz polish and memory hints for every kana

Jul 31, 2026

Added

  • Memory hints now cover every kana, not just the basic hiragana. Katakana and the voiced, combined, and extended kana all get keyword-and-story hints, most with two or three alternate images to pick from. Composed kana (like きゃ or ヴァ) also show an always-on Structure note that spells out how the piece is built from its parts, separate from the memory hint itself.
  • Encouragement as you build a streak in Reading mode. Answer a run of reading questions correctly (in the quiz or in flashcard practice) and the reveal greets you with a short, escalating line of encouragement instead of a flat "Correct!". A gentle note marks a broken streak.
  • The writing quiz shows you how the answer is written. When you reveal a writing-quiz answer, its stroke-order animation now plays in the corner (the same demo free practice uses), so a missed character teaches the fix, not just the finished shape.
  • An Undo button in the writing quiz. The bottom bar's Back button is now Undo, which reverts just your last stroke so a slip on a many-stroke character no longer means clearing the whole thing.
  • Sort your writing-quiz results, and retake the ones that need work. The end-of-quiz summary can now order its items by score (lowest or highest first), and a new button re-quizzes every character or word you scored below a target you set right there (default 80, with quick-pick 60/70/80/90 chips). It is the writing counterpart to Reading mode's "retake the ones I missed".
  • A clearer "Add new set" button on the Practice sets tab (was a plain "New").

Changed

  • The Quiz-me "Practice from" list now shows only the sets in the script you're currently studying, matching the Library's Sets tab.

0.3.0 Dark mode, memory hints, and a Practice sets tab

Jul 27, 2026

Added

  • A dark mode. New Appearance setting at the bottom of the Options (Study-scope) sheet: System / Light / Dark. It stays Light until you choose, so nothing changes on update. Dark is a warm, paper-like night theme (not a harsh inversion): the writing worksheet itself darkens, with the ghost glyph and grid dimmed to match, and your brush gets a separate ink for dark mode (a light chalk by default) so strokes stay legible on the dark sheet. The same brush-ink row edits whichever appearance is active. System follows your phone's own dark-mode setting. Score tints, the score-feedback card, and every Library surface get dark-appropriate colors too. (Also fixes a latent bug where the status-bar icons could turn invisible on phones set to system dark mode.)
  • Memory hints for the basic hiragana. Every one of the 46 basic hiragana now carries a keyword and a short story that tie its shape to its sound. A "How to remember" chip appears in writing practice and on flashcard and quiz answer reveals, and a "Hint" button offers the story before you answer (text-only in the writing quiz, since the shape is the answer). Each kana ships with alternates, so you can open "More ways to remember" and promote whichever image sticks; the choice is saved per character. It all sits behind a Memory hints switch in Options (on by default) and never affects your scores.
  • Practice sets now have their own tab. The sets you curate, plus the built-in starter sets, live on a dedicated Sets tab at the top of the Library beside Characters and Words, scoped to the script you're studying. A New button starts a Character set or Word set, and the top-bar search filters sets by name. This replaces the old "Add practice set" / "See sets" buttons in Options.

0.2.1 Undo, and practice-sheet sizes that earn their names

Jul 24, 2026

Added

  • Undo your latest stroke: a new Undo button next to Clear in writing practice reverts just the last stroke you drew, so one slip doesn't cost the progress on the rest of the character or word.

Changed

  • The repetition layouts are now sizes — XL, L, M, S (replacing 1×1/2×2/3×3, whose grid names meant nothing for words). XL is one big scored copy — the default, and what the quiz uses. L, M, and S tile the sheet with progressively smaller copies, and every step down is guaranteed to give you both visibly smaller cells and strictly more copies to practice on — for single characters and words of any length. Your saved layout carries over (old 2×2 becomes L, old 3×3 becomes S).
  • Repetition sheets now fill the canvas with complete rows instead of a fixed 4 or 9 slots — no more ragged, half-empty corner, and taller screens simply get more practice squares. Cells never shrink below a writable minimum (~40dp) for very long words.

Handwriting rendering: fixed-width brush & Printed/Handwritten style

Jul 6, 2026

Added

  • Character style — Printed vs Handwritten — a persisted CharacterForm setting (selector in the Study-scope sheet). Printed keeps the typographic font ghost you read on signs and screens; Handwritten draws the guide ghost from the same KanjiVG strokes the demo and scorer use (a grey brush), for both practice and the quiz. Rendering only — it never changes what is studied, demonstrated, or scored.
  • A "character X of Y" / "word X of Y" position line under the reading in the practice guide panel (the item's place in the active-script scoped list).

Changed

  • The stroke-order demo now renders as a bold, glyph-proportional brush (previously a thin 3.5 dp wireframe), matching the printed form of the glyph.
  • User ink is now a uniform fixed-width brush — dropped pen-pressure (finger contact size), speed thinning, and head/tail tapers, so a chubby finger and a thin stylus draw identically and the ink matches the demo. Width is the same fraction of the glyph as the demo at every size, so words, composite glyphs, and 2×2/3×3 grids thin automatically. Scoring is unaffected (it reads the raw points).
  • Removed the redundant Replay button from the stroke-order controls (Play already restarts the animation).

Full kana inventory (all hiragana + katakana)

Jul 4, 2026

Added

  • Extended kana (third opt-in) — small tsu (っ/ッ), small vowels, ゔ/ヴ, 22 foreign-sound katakana composites (ファ/ティ/ヴァ/シェ …), and obsolete ゐゑ/ヰヱ. Completes the inventory: 113 hiragana + 136 katakana, ~397 words.
  • Yōon (second opt-in) — contracted syllables (きゃ/しゅ/きょ …), the app's first composite characters (2-code-point glyphs whose strokes are composed from a base kana + a small ゃ/ゅ/ょ). KanaUnits groups a base + small kana into one reading unit.
